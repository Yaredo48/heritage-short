# EXPERIMENTS

One change at a time, the same seed where a model has one, with the result measured or saved. Files named here are in `outputs/`, and each model call is in `runs.jsonl`. Dates: 25 Nov 2026.

| # | Experiment | Hypothesis | What changed | Result (evidence) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | Cultural object names in image prompts | FLUX.2 klein knows the word "jebena" | Smoke prompt "a clay coffee pot (jebena) on a wooden table", seed 1 | **No.** It drew a plain terracotta vase with no spout or handle (`smoke/flux_klein_512.png`) | Describe objects by shape in every prompt ("round-bellied black clay coffee pot with a spout and a handle"); changelog v1 |
| E02 | Voice style vs narration length | Dropping "spoken slowly … gentle pauses" shortens the narration without losing warmth | Only `voice.style`: → "warm and tender, at a calm natural pace"; same voice (Charon), same text | 3 of 9 lines before the TTS quota ran out (F04): s01 6.31→5.23 s, s02 10.07→8.16 s, s03 6.83→7.03 s; **−12 % in total, but not consistent** (s03 got longer). TTS has no seed, so take-to-take variance is unknown | Not adopted: one complete take beats a mix of two styles. Retake files kept in `narration/raw/` for a listening comparison |
| E03 | Removing long pauses: FFmpeg vs own code | FFmpeg `silenceremove` can cap the pauses the TTS inserted | `silenceremove=stop_periods=-1:stop_duration=0.35:stop_silence=0.35` at −45/−40/−35 dB, rms and peak; then `audio.compress_pauses` (20 ms RMS frames, cut silences > max) | s02 (4 pauses of 0.63–1.13 s): FFmpeg best 10.07→9.30 s; own code 10.07→8.22 s. All 9 lines at 0.35 s: FFmpeg 77.4→75.3 s, **own code 77.4→65.3 s** | Own 60-line compressor; deterministic and tested (`test_compress_pauses_shortens_long_silence`) |
| E04 | How short can pauses get? | A cap near 0.45 s gets under 90 s and still sounds like breathing | `voice.max_pause_s` 0.35 / 0.45 / 0.55, no model calls | Total narration 65.3 / 67.9 / 70.0 s; at 0.45 the film is **84.4 s** (`make timing`) | 0.45 s. To confirm by native-speaker ears in user test 1 |
| E05 | Hiding the face while consent is pending | "From behind and slightly to the side, face hidden by a shawl" hides the face | s04 `no_face_prompt`, seed 104 | **Profile visible**: eye and nose (`keyframes/s04_642fa6f9145d.png`); also poured coffee into a cooking pot. Rewritten "seen from directly behind, her back to the camera": face hidden (`s04_2b30675fb308.png`) | "Directly behind / back to the camera" for every no-face shot; every character keyframe checked by eye (F07) |
| E06 | Describing the coffee pot | A shape description gets a recognisable pot | s04, seed 104: "round-bellied … long narrow neck and a spout" → "small round black clay coffee pot with a short spout"; "handleless cups" | "Long narrow neck" became a **pot with a long pipe**; the short-spout wording gave a plausible pot (`s04_f76730172c2f.png`). "Handleless" was ignored: cups still have handles | Keep the short-spout wording; 3 attempts per shot, then move on. Ask the family what her actual cup looks like |
| E07 | Style reference image vs text style block | Conditioning every keyframe on one approved style sheet (`input_image_0`) makes the look more consistent | 4 style-sheet candidates (seeds 201–204), chose 201 (most jebena-like pot, chipped cup). Then s02 and s05 with `use_style_reference: true`, same prompts and seeds | **Worse.** s02 pasted the sheet's table, pot and cup into the forest (`s02_83f6334168a8.png`); s05 lost the cream/green dress and the lamp, and a child's face appeared (`s05_09b7dd26d90e.png`). Klein treats an input image as content to edit, not as style | `use_style_reference: false`; consistency comes from the shared style block. (A character sheet, once consent exists, is still to be tested: it would be used *as* content) |
| E08 | Subtitles on a vertical frame | Greedy 32-character wrapping keeps lines readable | `textwrap` at 32 chars, 2 lines per cue | Produced an **orphan one-word cue** ("story.") on s01 | Balanced wrapping (fewest cues, even line lengths), 36 chars at 48 px on 1080-wide; tested in `test_subtitle_cues_fit_the_frame` |
| E09 | Loudness: hitting −14 LUFS and −1 dBTP after AAC | A two-pass linear loudnorm at −1 dBTP lands on target | Same mix, 4 variants, each encoded to AAC 192k and measured | TP −1.0: −14.90 LUFS, **−0.77 dBTP** (AAC added 0.2 dB of peak). TP −1.5: **−15.03 LUFS** (linear gain capped by peaks), −1.33 dBTP. TP −1.5 + limiter −8 dBFS: −14.66 / −1.23. **TP −1.5 + limiter −10 dBFS: −14.04 LUFS / −1.76 dBTP** | `mix.pre_limiter_db = −10`, `true_peak_db = −1.5`. To check by ear that ~6 dB of peak limiting on the voice is not audible |
| E10 | Does the ducking work? | `sidechaincompress` (threshold 0.02, ratio 6, attack 300 ms, release 1500 ms) lowers the music only under speech | Rendered the music stem with and without the sidechain; RMS inside each narration line vs pauses over 2 s | Without: −30.2 dBFS under speech, −31.3 in pauses. **With: −43.2 dBFS under speech (−13 dB), −31.3 in pauses (unchanged).** Only one pause is over 2 s (s06), so the pause figure rests on one window | Keep the brief's suggested settings; judge the overall music level (−8 dB) in user test 1 |
| E15 | Subtitle reading speed (26 Nov) | Cues split in proportion to their length and timed to the narration stay readable on a phone (common guidance: at most about 15–20 characters per second for adults, at least about 1 s on screen) | No change: measured the cues `assemble.cues()` produces for the current timeline, for all three widths (vertical 36, horizontal 42, SRT 42 characters) | Vertical: 15 cues, mean 10.5 cps, **max 13.8 cps**. Horizontal and SRT: 14 cues, mean 10.6 cps, max 13.8 cps. No cue above 17 cps; shortest cue on screen 3.32 s (s03, first half) | Keep. Added `test_subtitle_reading_speed` (≤ 17 cps, ≥ 1 s) so a later narration or wrapping change cannot make subtitles unreadable |
